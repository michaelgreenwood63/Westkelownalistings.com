Lake-View Properties: What to Actually Check

"Lake view" is one of the loosest terms in real estate listings. It can mean a full, unobstructed panoramic from every room, or it can mean a narrow sliver of water visible between two neighbouring rooflines from one specific spot on the deck. Both get marketed the same way. Here's what's actually worth checking before the view factors into your offer.

See it yourself, at the actual listed price point

Photos get taken with wide lenses from the best available angle, often from a ladder or drone, not from where you'll actually be standing or sitting day to day. Walk the property and look from the rooms and outdoor spaces you'll actually use — the kitchen, the primary bedroom, the deck at table height, not just the listing photo's vantage point. A view that only exists from a second-floor window is a very different thing than one you see from the living room couch.

Check what's actually on the lot in front of you

A view across an empty or low-built lot today isn't guaranteed to stay that way. Before you factor a view into your offer, it's worth checking what's actually zoned for the lots between you and the water — what height and density is permitted, not just what's currently built. West Kelowna's zoning bylaw sets real limits on height and density by zone, and our full zoning guide breaks down what applies where. If a neighbouring lot could legally support a taller build than what's there now, that's worth knowing before the view becomes part of your purchase decision, not after.

Orientation matters as much as the view itself

Which direction a lake-view property faces changes the actual experience of living there — afternoon sun on a west-facing deck is a very different thing than a shaded morning-only view on a north-facing one. It also affects practical things like how much natural light interior rooms get through the year, and how exposed outdoor spaces are to wind coming off the water. Worth asking directly rather than assuming "lake view" implies a specific orientation, since it doesn't.

If you're close to the water itself

Properties near the lakeshore can fall under floodplain setback requirements that affect what you can build, renovate, or add in the future. Our zoning guide covers floodplain setbacks for buyers near the water — worth a read if the property you're considering sits close to the shoreline itself, not just has a view of it.

The bottom line

A genuine, durable lake view is a real asset worth paying for. The work is confirming it's actually what the listing implies — seen from where you'll really be, protected from being built out by what's legally permitted next door, and not carrying floodplain restrictions you didn't know about. None of that shows up in the photos.
