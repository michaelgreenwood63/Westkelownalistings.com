For Investors

Investing in West Kelowna Real Estate

What actually matters before you buy: short-term rental rules, leasehold considerations, the taxes that apply here, and which complexes are genuinely investor-friendly.

The Opportunity

A varied market, still priced below Kelowna

West Kelowna sits directly across the lake from Kelowna, with its own incorporated city government (since 2007), its own zoning bylaw, and a genuinely varied housing stock: freehold single-family neighbourhoods, over 50 researched strata complexes, and a significant share of leasehold communities on Westbank First Nation land that simply don't exist on the Kelowna side of the bridge.

That variety is exactly why it pays to know the specifics before buying as an investor. The rules, the taxes, and the rental potential are not the same from one complex to the next, sometimes not even between two buildings on the same street.

Read This Before You Plan an Airbnb

Short-term rental rules are stricter than most buyers assume

Under the City of West Kelowna's own Zoning Bylaw No. 0320 (Section 3.19), running a short-term rental generally requires the owner to be a principal resident, present overnight during the guest's stay. A pure "buy it and rent it out short-term without living there" strategy is not permitted on a typical residential lot, capped at one short-term rental use per parcel, a maximum of four bedrooms, and two adults per bedroom. BC's provincial short-term rental legislation applies on top of this municipal rule, not instead of it.

The Exception, Not the Rule

A small number of complexes are zoned for it

Rather than a specific, arbitrary number of buildings, West Kelowna's tourist commercial exemption covers a known set of purpose-built resort and waterfront properties where short-term rental is permitted by zoning. These are the exception, not the norm, and worth knowing by name rather than assuming any waterfront or resort-styled building qualifies.

Leasehold & WFN Land

Lower entry price, different fundamentals

A significant share of West Kelowna's strata inventory sits on leased Westbank First Nation land rather than standard freehold title, typically on long (often 99-year) ground leases. That usually means a lower purchase price than an equivalent freehold unit, but it changes the fundamentals an investor should actually underwrite: financing terms shift as the remaining lease term shortens, resale pools can be smaller since not every buyer or lender treats leasehold the same as freehold, and WFN multi-family developments are commonly governed by a Society or Homeowners Corporation rather than a standard BC Strata Corporation.

Taxes to Underwrite

What actually applies here

BC's Property Transfer Tax applies on every purchase, with West Kelowna falling inside the Central Okanagan Regional District, one of the province's designated Additional Property Transfer Tax areas: a 20% surtax on top of the standard tax for foreign nationals, foreign corporations, and taxable trustees. There's no first-time-buyer or newly-built exemption angle for a pure investment purchase the way there can be for an owner-occupier, so budget for the full amount.

BC's Speculation and Vacancy Tax is a separate, province-wide annual tax on residential property left vacant, applied in specific designated regions of the province. Whether and how it applies to a specific property depends on your residency status, how the property is used, and the current designated-area boundaries, which the province updates from time to time, so confirm current applicability directly with the BC government rather than assuming last year's rules still hold.

New Construction

Presale activity worth watching

New-build presale units come with their own considerations: deposit structures spread over the construction timeline, a completion date that can shift, and financing that typically needs to be arranged closer to closing rather than at the time of the initial deposit. A current example in West Kelowna is a 39-unit, 4-storey leasehold building under construction at 3645 Gosset Road in Westbank Centre, on Westbank First Nation land, targeting completion in November 2027.
