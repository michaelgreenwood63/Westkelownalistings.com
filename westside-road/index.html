West Kelowna · Neighbourhood

Westside Road

A scenic, winding coastal corridor stretching north from Highway 97 along the lake, taking in Traders Cove, Wilson's Landing, Fintry, and Killiney Beach.

About & History

Not one neighbourhood — several pockets along one road.

Officially named "Westside Road/Bear Creek Road" by the City of West Kelowna, this corridor is nothing like the suburban grid neighbourhoods further south. It's made up of several distinct pockets — Traders Cove, Wilson's Landing, Fintry, and Killiney Beach — strung along the winding shoreline. Expect a mix of high-end lakeshore estates with private docks, rustic cabins, modular lakefront communities, and acreage properties nestled against the mountains: a peaceful, rural, retreat-style lifestyle centred on the water and pine forests.

Lower Westside Road (near Traders Cove) is just 8-12 minutes from the Bennett Bridge and downtown Kelowna. The farther-north pockets — Fintry and Killiney Beach — take roughly 25-40 minutes, trading convenience for genuine remote tranquility.

Bear Creek Provincial Park sits right at the start of the corridor — established in 1981 and expanded to its current 178 hectares (440 acres) in 1988, it's long been one of the more heavily used regional parks in the Okanagan (BC Parks recorded roughly 240,000 visits in a 2017–18 reporting period; current figures should be confirmed with BC Parks directly). Farther north, Fintry Provincial Park protects the heritage Fintry Estate and packing house, with trails leading up a dramatic waterfall stair climb and a boat launch.

Location & Geography

The northernmost neighbourhood, west of Highway 97.

Westside Road is the northernmost of West Kelowna's seven neighbourhoods — the last stop heading north from Glenrosa (the southernmost) through Smith Creek, Shannon Lake, and West Kelowna Estates. Unlike those more built-up pockets, Westside Road is defined by its length along the lakeshore, running north out of the main built-up area of the city toward Fintry.

There are no major grocery stores or pharmacies directly along the road itself — residents drive into West Kelowna Estates, Westbank Centre, or across to Kelowna for standard errands. That's part of the trade-off for direct lakeshore and park-adjacent living.

Campsites at Peak Season

Established in 1981 and expanded to its current size in 1988, Bear Creek is managed by BC Parks and offers lakeside camping, picnic areas, and trails that loop around the creek and surrounding canyon.

Schools

No school directly on the corridor.

Because the corridor is rural and spread out, students commute by school bus to nearby catchments in Central Okanagan School District #23: Mar Jok Elementary (K–5, the main primary school for lower Westside Road), Hudson Road Elementary (K–5, French Immersion), Constable Neil Bruce Middle (6–8), and Mount Boucherie Secondary (9–12).

The Drive

Narrow & Winding

Westside Road is famously narrow and cliffside in stretches. Road improvements have helped, but winter driving — dark, icy, or stormy conditions — requires extra care.

Homes along the mountain slope sit in a dense forest interface, so FireSmart principles, property clearance, and wildfire evacuation awareness are essential parts of living here.
