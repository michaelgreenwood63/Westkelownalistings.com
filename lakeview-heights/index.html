Lakeview Heights

Vineyard-covered benches above Okanagan Lake, with roots dating to the early-to-mid 19th century and some of West Kelowna's most sought-after view lots.

History

One of the city's oldest-rooted communities.

Lakeview Heights is one of the handful of West Kelowna neighbourhoods — alongside Glenrosa — with history dating back to the early-to-mid 19th century. Long before "West Kelowna" existed as a name, this bench above the lake was already being settled, with orchards and riding stables taking root alongside the earliest homes.

That agricultural history carried straight through to today: the benches are still home to working orchards, vineyards, and wineries woven directly into the residential streets, giving the neighbourhood a working-landscape character that's rare this close to a major lake. A resident-run Community Association still represents the neighbourhood's interests, and a standalone Lakeview Heights Community Hall anchors local gatherings — the kind of small-town civic life that's increasingly rare inside a growing city.

West Kelowna has kept investing here, too — a new Lakeview Heights fire hall, a roughly $14.3 million project, officially opened in October 2025.

Location & Geography

The lake-view bench on the city's north side.

The City of West Kelowna groups its neighbourhoods into two clusters. Lakeview Heights sits in the northern cluster, alongside West Kelowna Estates and Westside Road — as distinct from the southern cluster (Westbank Centre, Glenrosa, Shannon Lake, Smith Creek), which holds roughly half the city's population.

Highway 97C (the Okanagan Connector) and the William R. Bennett Bridge keep it within easy reach of both downtown Kelowna and Westbank's day-to-day services, despite its elevated, away-from-it-all feel.

The neighbourhood sits just south of Tsinstikeptum Indian Reserve No. 10, one of the Westbank First Nation's two self-governing reserves — a reminder that land tenure in this part of West Kelowna isn't always straightforward fee-simple ownership. Amenities

What's close by

Shopping

The Anders Store shopping centre covers day-to-day errands without leaving the neighbourhood.

Recreation

Sports fields, tennis courts, and lake-access parks, plus the wineries themselves, which double as a tourism draw for residents and visitors alike.

Safety & Civic Life

A newly built fire hall (opened October 2025) and a standalone Community Hall supporting an active resident association.

Getting Around

A short drive to Westbank Centre's broader retail and dining, and across the bridge to downtown Kelowna.
